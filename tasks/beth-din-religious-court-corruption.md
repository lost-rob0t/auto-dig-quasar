# Deep Auto-Dig: Beth Din Corruption / Religious Court Abuse

Go feral on the records, not on people as a class.

## Target

Primary target: documented corruption, abuse, coercion, conflicts of interest, financial misconduct, procedural misconduct, retaliation, cover-ups, and accountability failures involving Beth Din / batei din.

Secondary target: comparable documented corruption or abuse in other religious courts only after the Beth Din pass is deep enough to stand on its own.

This is not a race or ethnicity investigation. Do not generalize from specific courts, judges, rabbis, litigants, or institutions to Jews, Jewish communities, or religious people generally. Track institutions, actors, decisions, money, complaints, litigation, sanctions, and receipts.

## Enumerate

Find and normalize:

- Beth Din / tribunal names and aliases;
- judges/dayanim, administrators, staff, attorneys, intermediaries, and repeat players;
- parent organizations, funding/governance relationships, and institutional affiliations;
- significant disputes, arbitration proceedings, get/divorce proceedings, conversion disputes, commercial/family matters, and communal disciplinary matters tied to alleged misconduct;
- civil courts reviewing, enforcing, or vacating Beth Din decisions;
- prosecutors, regulators, disciplinary bodies, and appellate courts involved;
- comparable faith-based tribunals only for governance comparison after the primary Beth Din corpus is established.

## Misconduct categories

Prioritize evidence of:

- bribery, kickbacks, improper payments, self-dealing, or suspicious fee flows;
- undisclosed conflicts of interest or repeat-player favoritism;
- coercion, threats, retaliation, blacklisting, social pressure, or extortionate conduct;
- abuse of get/divorce proceedings;
- ex parte contacts, sham hearings, denial of notice, inability to present evidence, inconsistent procedures, or recusal failures;
- fabricated, altered, concealed, or selectively presented evidence;
- witness or complainant intimidation;
- suppression of complaints or retaliation against critics;
- criminal conduct by judges/dayanim, administrators, fixers, or intermediaries;
- civil judgments, vacatur decisions, sanctions, contempt findings, injunctions, disciplinary actions, or appellate criticism tied to tribunal conduct;
- repeated allegations against the same actors or institution from independent sources;
- governance failures such as opaque appointments, no meaningful appeal, financial opacity, weak recusal rules, unpublished procedure, or missing complaint mechanisms.

## Evidence discipline

Evidence priority:

1. court opinions, dockets, filings, judgments, injunctions, sanctions, arbitration-vacatur decisions, and appellate opinions;
2. indictments, plea agreements, convictions, sentencing records, law-enforcement releases, and regulator records;
3. bar, judicial, rabbinical, professional, charity, corporate, and nonprofit disciplinary/registry records;
4. official Beth Din rules, arbitration agreements, fee schedules, recusal rules, governance documents, published decisions, and archived versions;
5. sworn declarations, depositions, exhibits, correspondence, authenticated recordings, and documentary evidence;
6. high-quality investigative reporting with named sources and primary-document links;
7. first-person allegations and community reporting as leads only until corroborated.

Every claim must be tagged:

- **proven/adjudicated** — conviction, judgment, sanction, disciplinary finding, or equivalent authoritative result;
- **documented** — strongly supported by primary records but not finally adjudicated;
- **alleged** — claimed by a party/source and not independently established;
- **disputed** — materially contested by credible evidence or a named opposing party;
- **unverified lead** — worth digging but presently unsupported.

Do not mutate allegations into facts just because the allegation is spicy.

## Case ledger schema

For every substantive case/event, record:

- stable case ID;
- tribunal/institution;
- jurisdiction/location;
- people and organizations involved;
- date range;
- proceeding type;
- misconduct category;
- precise factual claim;
- claim status;
- strongest primary source;
- supporting sources;
- response/denial from accused party if available;
- court or disciplinary outcome;
- sanctions/remedies;
- financial amounts where documented;
- related cases/events;
- confidence score;
- unresolved questions.

## Pattern tests

After enumeration, test for actual patterns:

- recurring actors across nominally separate tribunals;
- repeat litigants or counsel receiving unusual treatment;
- undisclosed ties or conflicts;
- unusual fee flows;
- clusters of vacated awards;
- repeated coercion or retaliation allegations from independent complainants;
- procedural rules applied inconsistently;
- institutions lacking meaningful recusal, appeal, or complaint mechanisms;
- civil courts repeatedly criticizing the same tribunal or people;
- organizations steering disputes toward tribunals they influence financially or institutionally;
- complaints predating later proven misconduct.

Do not call one ugly case a systemic pattern. Make the evidence earn the conclusion.

## Comparative pass

Only after the Beth Din corpus is mature, enumerate comparable documented cases involving:

- Islamic/Sharia councils or tribunals;
- Christian ecclesiastical courts;
- canon-law tribunals;
- other faith-based arbitration bodies.

The comparison is for governance patterns, not religion-bashing.

## Required output

Produce a deep dossier with:

1. executive findings — strongest verified findings only;
2. Beth Din institution map;
3. case ledger;
4. actor ledger;
5. proven misconduct;
6. credible documented allegations separated from proven findings;
7. procedural/governance weaknesses;
8. money/conflicts map;
9. civil-court review and enforcement/vacatur history;
10. comparative religious-court cases;
11. contradictory evidence and defenses;
12. open questions / next dig targets;
13. source table with URL/document, source type, date, primary/secondary status, and reliability notes.

## Hard rules

- Facts first. Receipts first.
- No race angle.
- No collective blame.
- No anonymous accusation treated as evidence.
- No padding with generic criticism of religion.
- Include exculpatory evidence, defenses, reversals, and corrections.
- Distinguish private arbitration from state judicial authority.
- Resolve exact institutions before merging records under generic names like "Beth Din."
- Preserve provenance for every material assertion.
- Prefer primary documents even when they ruin the dramatic version of the story.
- Do not dox private people or collect irrelevant home/family data.

## Completion condition

Complete only when the dig has enumerated the major publicly documented Beth Din corruption/misconduct cases discoverable across multiple jurisdictions, resolved recurring institutions and actors, separated proven conduct from allegations, identified patterns supported by multiple independent cases, and produced a source-complete dossier with unresolved leads clearly marked.
